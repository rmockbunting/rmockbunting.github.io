01 · About

The person behind the prose.

René Mock-Bunting is a senior technical writer, editor, and documentation manager with more than 20 years of experience delivering high-impact technical, regulatory, and research-based documentation for government, healthcare, SaaS, engineering, and enterprise teams. She is based in Wilmington, North Carolina, and works remotely with clients across the U.S. and the Pacific.

Her work centers on the parts of documentation that quietly decide whether a project ships on time and passes review: information architecture, structured authoring, document lifecycle management, and the compliance frameworks (HIPAA, PII, ITIL) that sit underneath them. She has led documentation work for benefits eligibility systems serving state-government clients, a statewide IaaS cloud platform, and a regulated life-sciences product line — all environments where accuracy is non-negotiable and the audit trail has to hold together.

René works comfortably in agile, asynchronous, cross-functional teams, and is at her best translating dense subject-matter expertise into language that a first-day hire, a regulator, or a busy executive can actually use. She holds a B.A. in English from Penn State and graduate studies in Biology from Miami University — a combination that has, more than once, been the difference between a doc that ships and one that gets sent back.

04 · Editing case studies

Problems solved.

All case studies are anonymized and shared with client permission.
/Case 01

Document control program for a statewide cloud platform

Statewide IaaS implementation — 2017 – 2019
Problem

Documentation for a multi-year statewide Infrastructure-as-a-Service implementation had drifted out of compliance with project deadlines. Review and approval cycles were untracked, the SharePoint repository was fragmented across teams, and governance, cybersecurity, and infrastructure support content all lived in different places with different standards.

Approach

Took over document-control operations and designed a new Kanban-tracked review and approval process from the ground up. Consolidated the SharePoint repository, established version control and submission workflows, and worked with international SMEs to bring governance, cybersecurity, and infrastructure documentation up to a single client-aligned standard.

Outcome

Compliance with project deadlines was restored and document visibility improved across the program. The Kanban-based review process became the operating model for the program and the basis for subsequent documentation standards René would build for later engagements.

/Case 02

Confluence-based submission & review workflow for a benefits eligibility program

State Department of Human Services engagement — 2019 – 2021
Problem

Engineering and software-development deliverables across a benefits eligibility program were inconsistent in structure, hard to trace for audit, and dependent on a small set of senior reviewers — creating a documentation bottleneck that threatened compliance and shipping timelines.

Approach

Designed and implemented a new document submission and review workflow in Confluence, paired with style guides, templates, and training resources. Trained global SMEs on the new documentation standards and best practices, and enforced the QA review process across engineering and software-development deliverables.

Outcome

Audit traceability improved materially, the team could move faster without losing compliance posture, and the program maintained an audit-ready documentation repository throughout the engagement. The workflow became the template René later reused on the state DHS portfolio at her next engagement.

/Case 03

Staff-level documentation program for a regulated life-sciences product line

Life-sciences instrument company — 2021 – 2023
Problem

Following a promotion to Staff level, René inherited end-to-end documentation ownership for a regulated life-sciences product line — a complex mix of engineering hardware, software, and regulated scientific protocols. The team had no shared documentation standards, no scalable review process, and no consistent approach to regulatory traceability.

Approach

Owned the full document lifecycle — planning, authoring, editing, review cycles, regulatory approval, and publishing via a regulated PLM system. Authored user manuals, protocols, and validation documents verified through simulation software and direct lab use, and established team-wide documentation standards, templates, and a Confluence-based document-control process that the team could actually maintain.

Outcome

Documentation consistency and scalability improved across the product line, the team had a working submission and approval process for the first time, and the program remained in compliance with life-sciences regulatory standards through a period of rapid product growth.

05 · How I work

Four principles.

01

Structure before prose

Most documentation problems are information-architecture problems in disguise. I get the structure and the submission workflow right first, and the writing gets easier — and more accurate — from there.

02

Plain language, technical rigor

Clarity is not a concession to the reader. It is what rigorous thinking looks like on the page. The best technical documents are the ones a new contributor, a regulator, and a busy executive can all use without translation.

03

Standards that survive handoffs

A style guide only matters if the next contributor uses it. I build documentation programs around the workflows people actually work in — Confluence spaces, Arena PLM, SharePoint — and pair every standard with a template and a checklist.

04

Audit-ready by default

In regulated environments, traceability is not an add-on. I design submission, review, and version-control processes so the audit trail is the natural byproduct of doing the work — not a separate deliverable.
